2. Overview

This service is an application programming interface (API) for software and AI agents to retrieve data in exchange for small stablecoin payments (USDC). This website has no user accounts, registration, cookies, tracking, analytics tools, or externally loaded fonts or scripts. We process only what is necessary for the request and payment. The service is intended for businesses (see the terms of use); the following information nevertheless applies to everyone whose data is processed.

3. Hosting and connection data

Hosting: The service runs on a virtual server provided by Hetzner Online GmbH, Industriestr. 25, 91710 Gunzenhausen, Germany, at the Falkenstein data center in Germany. A data processing agreement under Article 28 GDPR is in place with the provider. The legal basis is Article 6(1)(f) GDPR (secure and efficient operation of our service).

IP addresses: Your IP address must briefly be processed so that the response can reach you. We do not store IP addresses persistently. To prevent overload and abuse (per-minute request limits and blocking after repeated failed logins to the operator area), the address (only the network prefix for IPv6) is held only in memory for a few minutes and then discarded; it is neither written to disk nor logged. Free sample: To limit free samples to one per user and service per day, the address (network prefix for IPv6) is held only in memory until the end of the day (UTC) and discarded on service restart. The input of a sample is treated like other request inputs (section 4). Wishlist: For the free wishlist (up to three submissions per user per day), the address (network prefix for IPv6) is also held only in memory until the end of the day (UTC) and discarded on restart. Legal basis: Article 6(1)(f) GDPR (availability and security).

Web server access logs: Our reverse proxy (Caddy) logs the time, method, requested path, status code, response size and duration. No IP addresses, headers or request bodies are logged. Logs rotate by size (three files of 10 MB each) and are therefore overwritten after a short time. Legal basis: Article 6(1)(f) GDPR (troubleshooting and security).

Server administration: Only the operator can access the server via SSH. Operating system logs may contain failed third-party login attempts together with IP addresses (attack prevention, Article 6(1)(f) GDPR). These system logs are rotated automatically and deleted after four weeks at the latest.

Encryption: Connections to this website and the API use TLS (HTTPS) encryption (Article 32 GDPR).

4. API usage

For a request, we process the input (such as search terms, domain, country and language), the result, time, price and a random request ID. For Keyword Ideas, a single seed phrase is sent to DataForSEO for a word-based Keyword Suggestions query. The purposes are to fulfill the purchased request, provide the result again if a response is lost, and allow one free retry if the data provider fails. Legal basis: Article 6(1)(b) GDPR (performance of a contract) or Article 6(1)(f).

Retention: Inputs and results are automatically deleted after 30 days. Identical requests may be cached briefly in memory (up to seven days, without reference to a person). For recovery after technical failures, we keep up to 14 daily backup copies on the same server. Expired inputs and results are also deleted from these copies every six hours.

Optional buyer example: If you run the downloadable example code yourself, it creates a private purchase file on your computer containing the request, status and, where available, response and result token. This file is not uploaded to us as a file. Private wallet keys and payment signatures are not stored in it. It is part of your local application and subject to your own retention policy; the 30-day deletion period above concerns our server-side storage.

Wishlist (optional and free): You may submit a short text (10 to 300 characters) describing a data tool you would like to use. We store only the text and time, without linking it to your IP address, wallet or identity. Texts are not public, are read only by the operator and are automatically deleted after 30 days. No reply is provided. Legal basis: Article 6(1)(f) GDPR (product improvement). Please do not include personal data.

Please do not submit personal data as input. The service is intended for search terms, domains and country codes.

5. x402 payments and blockchain

Payments use USDC on the Base blockchain through the x402 protocol. We process the payer's public wallet address, amount, time, network, USDC contract, recipient address, public payment identifier (nonce), authorization expiry and transaction identifier (transaction hash). Wallet addresses are pseudonyms; we do not assign them to a person unless additional information is available. If the payment outcome is uncertain, we retain the payment record for review. An automatic, read-only blockchain reconciliation looks for confirmed payments; it triggers neither new payments nor data requests. For this reconciliation, public wallet addresses and payment identifiers are sent to the public Base RPC endpoints (mainnet.base.org, or sepolia.base.org in test mode). No API inputs, payment signatures or user IP addresses are transmitted. Short-lived protective locks for locally verified payer addresses are held only in memory. Purposes: payment processing, refunds, bookkeeping and evidence. Legal basis: Article 6(1)(b) GDPR and, for retention, Article 6(1)(c) GDPR (commercial and tax obligations).

Retention: We retain these payment data for the statutory retention periods (currently eight years for accounting records and six years for commercial and business correspondence).

Blockchain: Blockchain transactions are public and permanent. We cannot change or delete them and have no control over these data.

Payment facilitator: Payment authorizations are checked and executed through the Coinbase group's CDP Facilitator (Coinbase, Inc., USA, or affiliated companies). Payment authorization, wallet address and amount are transmitted; the provider also performs sanctions and risk checks. Legal basis: Article 6(1)(b) and (f) GDPR. According to the provider, transfers to the USA rely on the EU-US Data Privacy Framework or standard contractual clauses. In test mode, a public test facilitator is used instead and no real money changes hands.
